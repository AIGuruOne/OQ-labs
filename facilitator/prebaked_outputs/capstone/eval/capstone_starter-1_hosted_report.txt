READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING A NUMBER
  - 20 tickets: one ticket is 5 percentage points. A gap of 10 points between two runs is two
    tickets - do not read it as a trend.
  - Thin classes, fewer than 5 tickets each: category=hardware (n=3), category=software (n=3),
    category=network (n=3), category=erp (n=2), category=telecom (n=2), category=other (n=2),
    urgency=medium (n=4), urgency=high (n=4), urgency=critical (n=2), impact=team (n=4), impact=site
    (n=1), impact=enterprise (n=1). Their per-class scores are anecdotes, not measurements. No file
    in this repo has enough rare-class tickets to prove rare-class quality - val.jsonl included.
  - requested_action: It compares WORDS, not meaning, and the expected clauses are written in one
    house style. 'Reset password' vs 'Reset the user's MyPortal password' scores 0.33 though a desk
    agent would do the same thing; 'Reset the password' vs 'Do not reset the password' scores 0.60.
    Treat the match rate as a floor - it rewards house wording - and read the sample below the
    threshold (rubric H2).
  - Labels follow written rules (corpus/README.md, 'Labelling rules'). Where a rule is arguable -
    mostly urgency - a 'miss' may be a defensible answer. Rubric H1 handles that.
  - One run at temperature 0. Local models can still vary slightly between runs and machines; hosted
    models change under you. Re-run before quoting a number.
