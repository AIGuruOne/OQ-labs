# Peer scoring sheet (Day 5, S30 showcase, 65 minutes)

**Who scores.** Every person scores every build except one they worked
on, alone, in silence, straight after that demo. **Scores are not
discussed while scoring.** Questions are about the build, never about a
score. The facilitator collects the sheets and reads the scores out at
the end.

**Small cohorts.** With three or four people in the room there may be
only one build, and then nobody is eligible under the rule above. Use
the one-build shape instead: **every member scores the build
independently and in silence, and the facilitator scores it too.** The
sheets are then revealed side by side, and where two scorers differ by two
on a criterion, that gap is the session — the anchors are written to be
visible from the audience, so a real disagreement means the build shows
one thing to one person and another to the next. Which shape you are in
was decided at the Day 1 close, when groups formed.

A demo that overruns stops at the bell; the group scores what was shown.
Each demo opens with the group's deployment-checklist verdict (one sentence).

**Close the lowest criterion live** (the four small-cohort rows only).
Take the criterion that scored worst across the room and fix it in the
room, on the build, with everyone watching. A 0 on criterion 3 becomes a
hostile input actually caught; a 0 on criterion 5 becomes an owner's name
and a cost with its source. This is the slot a big cohort never has, and
it is worth more than another demo: the week's rubric stops being paper
and becomes a change. If nothing scored below 2, spend it on criterion 6
instead — name the gaps and who closes each one.

## The six criteria (0 / 1 / 2 each, 12 in all)

Score what was **shown**, not what was said. When unsure between two
scores, take the lower one: the anchors are written so that a 2 is
visible from the audience.

| # | Criterion (the session it comes from) | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **It ran live** (S27-S28) | nothing ran, or only slides / screenshots | ran, but a FALLBACK or failure went unmentioned | ran live, and they said which pieces were theirs and which were pre-baked |
| 2 | **A number on unseen data** (S12, S19) | no number, or measured on training data | a number, but no comparison or no n | a number on held-out data, compared with a baseline, with its n and what it does not show |
| 3 | **A wrong answer has a path** (S24, S25) | the wrong case was not shown | described, not demonstrated | a wrong or hostile input shown being caught, refused or sent to a person |
| 4 | **Nothing writes without a person** (S26, S29) | a write happens with no approval, or nobody knows | writes are gated, but the approver or the log line was not shown | "no writes", or an approval shown with the named approver and its audit line |
| 5 | **Owned after Thursday** (S3-S5, S29) | no owner, no cost | owner or cost named, not both | a named owner, a cost per month with its source, and the model switch shown or explained |
| 6 | **Honest about the gaps** (all week) | claims more than was shown | gaps mentioned when asked | gaps named unprompted, each with who would close it |

## For the facilitator

- **Four or more scorers:** read out the **median** per criterion per
  build, not the total and not a ranking.
- **Two or three scorers,** which is the usual case at this cohort size:
  a median is noise. Read out **every score** and go straight to the
  gaps. Do not average two numbers and call it a result.
- A column of 0s on criterion 3 across the room is the finding to close
  the week on, not a group's failure.
- Keep the "steal" column: it is the list OQ takes home. With one build
  it becomes "one thing I would change first", which is the same list
  pointed inward.
- If two scorers differ by two on one criterion, the anchor was unclear for
  that demo. With a big cohort, note it for the next run and do not
  re-score. With three people you have the time, so argue it to a shared
  score in the reveal slot — the argument is where the criterion gets
  learned.
- Nobody has timed this session with a real group. The small-cohort rows
  are arithmetic, not measurement: watch the clock on the first demo and
  move the "close the lowest criterion" slot, which is the one with give.
